DIY Junk Hauling vs. Hiring a Pro in Anderson, SC: A Real Garage Cleanout Cost Breakdown

If you've got a garage full of junk in Anderson, SC and you're trying to decide whether to rent a truck and haul it yourself or just call a junk removal company, you've probably run into the same problem everyone else does: nobody gives you real numbers. Junk removal sites want you to book a job, and truck rental sites bury their real cost behind a "starting at $19.95" headline that almost nobody actually pays.

We dug into the actual costs — U-Haul's real pricing structure, Anderson County's landfill and disposal rules, and realistic time estimates — so you can do this math yourself before you decide. No sales pitch, just the numbers.

Scenario: Cleaning Out a Two-Car Garage

Let's use a realistic, common job: a two-car garage that's accumulated years of stuff — some old furniture, a broken lawnmower, boxes of miscellaneous junk, maybe an old mini-fridge, a few bags of yard waste. Call it roughly half a pickup truck bed's worth of volume, maybe more once it's all pulled out and stacked.

Step 1: What the U-Haul Actually Costs (Not the Advertised Price)

U-Haul advertises pickup trucks, cargo vans, and 10-foot box trucks at $19.95/day — but that's just the starting point. Here's what actually gets added:

Cost ItemReal Price
Base daily rate (pickup, cargo van, or 10' truck)$19.95

FuelYou return the tank full — budget $15–$25 for local driving
Optional damage coverage (Safemove/CDW)~$15–$30/day if you want it

For a garage cleanout, you're realistically looking at a round trip of somewhere between 10–20 miles depending on where you live in the Anderson area and which convenience center or landfill you're headed to. Do the math on a 15-mile round trip:

  • Base rate: $19.95
  • Mileage (15 mi × ~$1.00/mi average): ~$15.00
  • Environmental fee: ~$3.00
  • Fuel: ~$10–$15

Realistic total for the truck itself: roughly $48–$53 — not $19.95. If you add optional damage coverage, add another $15–$30. If your garage has more volume than a pickup can hold and you need the 15-foot truck instead, the daily rate jumps to $29.95 before mileage and fees.

Step 2: What Disposal Actually Costs in Anderson County

This is where it gets genuinely good news for the DIY route — if your junk qualifies.

The Starr C&D Landfill (390 Roy Arnold Rd, Starr, SC) lets Anderson County residents dump construction debris and land-clearing material completely free, with three exceptions: shingles ($33/ton), asbestos, and manufactured mobile homes ($300–$400 to demolish and dispose). If your garage cleanout includes old lumber, broken furniture frames, or general debris, this could cost you nothing at the gate.

If you've got old tires in that garage, Anderson County's Materials Recovery Facility gives residents their first 10 tires free, then charges $4.15 per passenger/light truck tire after that.

Here's the honest gap: we could not find a clearly published fee schedule for general household bulky items — old furniture, a non-working mini-fridge, general "stuff" — dropped off at Anderson County's regular convenience centers. Some counties charge nothing for this; some charge a surcharge specifically for appliances containing refrigerant (fridges, freezers, window AC units), often in the $15–$35 range, because federal law requires the refrigerant to be professionally recovered before disposal. Before you load up a fridge or freezer and drive it out there, call Anderson County Solid Waste at (864) 260-1001 to confirm there's no surcharge — it's a two-minute call that could save you a wasted trip.

Pros and Cons, Honestly

DIY makes sense when:

  • Your garage junk is mostly general debris that qualifies for free disposal at Starr Landfill
  • You have a free afternoon and someone to help you lift
  • You don't have appliances with refrigerant or shingles in the mix
  • You're comfortable driving a rental truck and returning it on time

Hiring a pro makes more sense when:

  • Your garage has heavy items — old appliances, a couch, exercise equipment — that are genuinely unsafe to lift alone
  • You're short on time and a half-day project isn't realistic this week
  • You'd rather pay for certainty than risk a surprise landfill surcharge or a second trip
  • You want everything gone in one visit instead of managing the sorting, loading, and driving yourself

Neither option is objectively "better" — it genuinely depends on what's in your garage and how much your afternoon is worth to you. If your load is mostly free-to-dump debris and you've got the time and the muscle, DIY can save you real money. If it's heavy, mixed, or you're pressed for time, the math tips toward hiring out pretty quickly once you account for your own hours.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it actually cost to rent a U-Haul for a garage cleanout in Anderson, SC?

Budget $48–$70+ for a local round trip once mileage, fuel, and the environmental fee are included — not the advertised $19.95.

Is dumping at the Anderson County landfill free?

Construction debris and land-clearing material are free for residents at the Starr C&D Landfill, except shingles ($33/ton), asbestos, and mobile homes. General household bulky items at the regular convenience centers may have different rules — call (864) 260-1001 to confirm before hauling large appliances.

What if my garage cleanout includes an old refrigerator or freezer?

Appliances containing refrigerant often carry a separate disposal surcharge due to federal refrigerant recovery requirements. Confirm the fee with Anderson County Solid Waste before your trip.

How long does a DIY garage cleanout actually take?

Realistically 3.5 to 5 hours solo, including truck pickup, loading, the drive to disposal, unloading, and returning the truck.

How long does it take if I hire a junk removal company instead?

Most standard residential jobs in the Anderson area take between 30 minutes and 2 hours once the crew arrives, since there's no truck logistics or driving involved on your end.
