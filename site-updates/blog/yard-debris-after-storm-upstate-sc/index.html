What to Do With Yard Debris After a Storm in Upstate SC

Upstate South Carolina gets its share of severe weather. Thunderstorms with high winds are common in spring and summer. Hurricane remnants occasionally push up from the coast. Ice storms in January or February can snap hardwood limbs and bring down trees that look perfectly healthy.

After the storm passes and you walk the yard, the question is: what do you actually do with all of it?

What You're Typically Dealing With After an Upstate Storm

Storm debris in the Anderson area generally falls into a few categories:

Small limbs and leaf debris — the minor cleanup. This can usually be handled with a standard yard waste cart, raked into piles, or bagged for curbside pickup (check your municipality's rules on yard waste bags).

Medium limbs (2–6 inches diameter) — these require a chainsaw or handsaw to cut into manageable sections. Once cut, they can be stacked in piles for haul-away or, if your municipality allows it, placed at the curb for bulk yard waste pickup.

Large fallen limbs or partial tree falls — this is where it gets complicated. A major limb or a partial tree can weigh hundreds of pounds. Cutting it is one thing; moving it is another. And if the limb fell near a structure, on a fence, or in an area with limited access, safe removal requires equipment and experience.

Debris on structures — if a limb or tree came down on your roof, garage, fence, or outbuilding, that's a different category of problem. Structural damage needs a licensed contractor to assess. Focus on clearing what you can safely reach; don't put yourself on a damaged roof to pull off debris.

Uprooted trees — full uproots with root balls attached are significant jobs. Root ball removal typically requires a stump grinder or excavator. This falls outside basic junk removal and into the tree service or land-clearing category.

Immediate Safety First

Before you start clearing anything, walk the yard and assess:

  • Look up. Hanging limbs — called "widow makers" — are the most dangerous outcome of a storm. A limb that's cracked but not yet fallen can come down without warning. Don't walk or work under hanging debris until you're sure it's stable.
  • Check for downed utility lines. If any lines came down — power, cable, phone — stay far away and call Duke Energy or your utility provider immediately. Never assume a downed line is dead.
  • Assess structural damage before you enter buildings. If a limb came down on a structure, inspect from outside before going in.
  • Chainsaw safety. Cutting under tension (a limb that's bent and loaded) can cause unexpected kickback. If you don't know how to read tension in a limb, don't start cutting.

Your Cleanup Options

Do it yourself — small to medium debris

For manageable debris, the DIY approach works fine:

  • Cut limbs into sections that fit in your vehicle, a yard waste cart, or municipal yard waste bags
  • Stack larger sections neatly for haul-away
  • Check your municipality's schedule for yard waste pickup — after major storms, some municipalities run additional passes

Hire a tree service — for large trees and structural situations

If you have a whole tree down or a limb that came down on a structure, hire a licensed tree service. This is different from junk removal — it involves cutting, rigging, and sometimes crane work. An arborist is the right call for anything involving a tree actively in contact with your home.

Hire a yard waste removal service — for the aftermath

Once the big cutting is done, you're often left with a large pile of sections, small branches, and debris that needs to go somewhere. That's where yard waste removal comes in. We handle brush piles, limb sections, storm debris cleanup, and general yard waste haul-away across Anderson and Upstate SC.

This is the most common call we get after a major storm: the homeowner (or a tree service) has already done the cutting, and now there's a 20-foot pile of sections and brush that needs to disappear.

What Anderson County and Municipalities Offer After Storms

After a declared disaster or particularly severe storm event, Anderson County or local municipalities may offer additional debris pickup. This varies by event — some storms trigger emergency pickup programs, others don't.

After a major storm, check:

  • Anderson County's official communications (website, social media)
  • City of Anderson public works announcements
  • Your municipality's standard yard waste pickup schedule — even without special programs, regular yard waste collection continues

The limitation of municipal programs: Even when special post-storm pickup is available, there are typically item restrictions and scheduling delays. If you need the yard cleared quickly — for safety, for a rental property, or because you're listing the home — the municipal timeline may not work for you.

Timing Your Cleanup

Upstate SC storm season peaks roughly May through September for thunderstorm events, with a secondary window in January and February for ice. But storms don't follow a calendar.

The best time to schedule storm debris removal is as soon as the debris is safe to access — typically 24 to 48 hours after a storm, once any hanging limbs have been assessed and dealt with. Waiting weeks means the debris can become a habitat issue (stacked wood attracts pests) or a neighbor relations issue.
