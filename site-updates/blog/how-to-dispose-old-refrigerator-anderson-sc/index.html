How to Dispose of an Old Refrigerator in Anderson, SC

You've got a new refrigerator coming. The old one is sitting in the kitchen, the garage, or on the porch. And now you're realizing that getting rid of a refrigerator isn't as simple as dragging it to the curb.

Here's what Anderson, SC homeowners need to know about refrigerator disposal — what the rules are, why they exist, and how to get it taken care of without a hassle.

Why You Can't Just Dump an Old Refrigerator

Refrigerators, window air conditioners, dehumidifiers, and other cooling appliances contain refrigerant — a chemical compound regulated under the EPA's Section 608 of the Clean Air Act.

The rule is straightforward: refrigerant must be recovered by a certified technician before the appliance is scrapped or disposed of. Venting refrigerant into the atmosphere is illegal. This applies to residential appliances, not just commercial equipment.

What this means practically: you cannot legally take a refrigerator to the landfill yourself in most cases, and you definitely can't leave it at the curb for someone to haul away unless you know for certain that person is certified to handle the refrigerant.

Your Refrigerator Disposal Options in Anderson, SC

Option 1: Contact Anderson County Solid Waste

Anderson County manages landfill operations and may have procedures for accepting refrigerant-containing appliances. Before you load the appliance into a truck, call Anderson County Solid Waste directly to confirm:

  • Whether they accept refrigerators and how (some facilities handle refrigerant recovery on-site)
  • What fees apply
  • Whether the appliance needs to be secured (doors removed for safety in some jurisdictions)

Don't assume the answer — policies and procedures at county solid waste facilities change, and it's worth the five-minute phone call.

Option 2: Utility Company Rebate and Pickup Programs

Duke Energy and other utilities periodically run appliance recycling programs in South Carolina that include free pickup of old refrigerators and freezers, sometimes with a rebate attached. These programs come and go, and eligibility requirements vary.

Check Duke Energy's current rebate and recycling offerings on their official site. If a program is available in your area, this can be the easiest and most economical path — free pickup, certified disposal, and money back.

Option 3: Appliance Retailers

When you buy a new refrigerator, ask the retailer whether they haul away the old one. Many major appliance retailers offer old appliance haul-away as part of delivery. There may be a fee, or it may be included.

This only works when you're buying a replacement at the same time, but it's worth asking about — it's often the most convenient option if you're already getting delivery.

Option 4: Hire a Professional Junk Removal Service

A professional appliance removal company has the legal authorization to handle refrigerant-containing appliances. We arrange for proper refrigerant recovery through certified channels before the appliance is scrapped. You don't need to manage that — it's part of the service.

This is the right call when:

  • You're not getting delivery of a new appliance (or the retailer doesn't offer haul-away)
  • The refrigerator is in a location that makes self-hauling difficult (basement, tight kitchen, second floor)
  • You have multiple appliances to remove at once
  • You need it gone quickly

What NOT to Do

Don't leave it at the curb for random pickup. If someone hauls your refrigerator away without recovering the refrigerant first, they're breaking federal law — and the refrigerant ends up in the atmosphere regardless.

Don't give it away on Facebook Marketplace or Craigslist if it doesn't work. Transferring a broken appliance to another private individual just moves the problem. They'll eventually face the same disposal issue, or it ends up illegally dumped.

Don't haul it to the landfill without calling ahead. Many landfills have specific requirements for refrigerant-containing appliances. Show up unannounced with a fridge and you may be turned away.

Don't leave it outside long-term. Old refrigerators are a safety hazard — children can become trapped inside. In South Carolina, state law requires that doors be removed from discarded refrigerators left on the property for any extended period.

What About Other Appliances?

The same refrigerant rules apply to:

  • Chest and upright freezers
  • Window and portable air conditioners
  • Dehumidifiers
  • Wine coolers and mini-fridges

Other appliances — washers, dryers, dishwashers, ovens, water heaters — don't contain refrigerants and have different disposal requirements. These are generally easier to dispose of, but still shouldn't go in regular trash due to their size and metal content.
