### Why You Can't Just Dump an Old Refrigerator

Refrigerators, window air conditioners, dehumidifiers, and other cooling appliances contain refrigerant — a chemical compound that was historically a hydrofluorocarbon (HFC) or, in older units, a hydrochlorofluorocarbon (HCFC). These chemicals are regulated under the EPA's Section 608 of the Clean Air Act.

The rule is straightforward: refrigerant must be recovered by a certified technician before the appliance is scrapped or disposed of. Venting refrigerant into the atmosphere is illegal. This applies to residential appliances, not just commercial equipment.

What this means practically: you cannot legally take a refrigerator to the landfill yourself in most cases (many landfills require proof that refrigerant has been removed, or they have their own certified equipment on-site). And you definitely can't leave it at the curb for someone to haul away unless you know for certain that person is certified to handle the refrigerant.

### Your Refrigerator Disposal Options in Anderson, SC

**Option 1: Contact Anderson County Solid Waste**

Anderson County manages landfill operations and may have procedures for accepting refrigerants-containing appliances. Before you load the appliance into a truck, call Anderson County Solid Waste directly to confirm:
- Whether they accept refrigerators and how (some facilities handle refrigerant recovery on-site)
- What fees apply
- Whether the appliance needs to be secured (doors removed for safety in some jurisdictions)

Don't assume the answer — policies and procedures at county solid waste facilities change, and it's worth the five-minute phone call.

**Option 2: Utility Company Rebate and Pickup Programs**

Duke Energy and other utilities periodically run appliance recycling programs in South Carolina that include free pickup of old refrigerators and freezers, sometimes with a rebate attached. These programs come and go, and eligibility requirements vary.

Check Duke Energy's current rebate and recycling offerings on their official site. If a program is available in your area, this can be the easiest and most economical path — free pickup, certified disposal, and money back.

**Option 3: Appliance Retailers**

When you buy a new refrigerator, ask the retailer whether they haul away the old one. Many major appliance retailers (Home Depot, Lowe's, Best Buy) offer old appliance haul-away as part of delivery. There may be a fee, or it may be included.

This only works when you're buying a replacement at the same time, but it's worth asking about — it's often the most convenient option if you're already getting delivery.

### What NOT to Do

**Don't leave it at the curb for random pickup.** If someone hauls your refrigerator away without recovering the refrigerant first, they're breaking federal law — and the refrigerant ends up in the atmosphere regardless. You may also face issues if a municipality cites you for improper disposal.

**Don't give it away on Facebook Marketplace or Craigslist if it doesn't work.** Transferring a broken appliance to another private individual doesn't solve the disposal problem — it just moves it. They'll eventually face the same disposal issue, or it ends up illegally dumped.

**Don't haul it to the landfill without calling ahead.** As noted above, many landfills have specific requirements for refrigerant-containing appliances. Show up unannounced with a fridge and you may be turned away.

**Don't leave it outside long-term.** Old refrigerators are a safety hazard — children can become trapped inside. In South Carolina, state law requires that doors be removed from discarded refrigerators. If it's sitting on your property for any extended period, take the door off or lock it.

### What About Other Appliances?

The same refrigerant rules apply to:
- Chest and upright freezers
- Window and portable air conditioners
- Dehumidifiers
- Wine coolers and mini-fridges

Other appliances — washers, dryers, dishwashers, ovens, water heaters — don't contain refrigerants and have different disposal requirements. These are generally easier to dispose of, but still shouldn't go in regular trash due to their size and metal content.
